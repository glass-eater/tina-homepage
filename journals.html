Becca Abbe - The Internet's Back to the Land Movement

It was kinda jarring to read that we imagined the internet as a place where we could all be equal. I understand this was the optimistic outlook we had for the internet in the 20th century, but we’ve experienced and learned of this “equalizer” as a double edged sword.

It’s like after a long day at work being tormented by your evil boss, you log onto the internet and you can be anything you want–like you can pretend you’re a cute girl to feel better about yourself– but you just want to roleplay as your evil boss and abuse others. The internet provides you the tools to explore and build new identities, but with this power, we can also recreate the same characters and systems that oppress us in reality. People grasp onto roles and force others into roles beneath them, etc. There is no utopia. In the same way that capitalism colonized the internet, society does the same.

I hate the idea that we have to choose and we have to stick to what we’re good at, or if we are good at it, we need to make money out of it. I feel that these are also cultural sentiments from capitalism that ultimately led to the state of the internet today. We let corporations, apps and platforms tip the scale on human creators because it’s easier to defer to a big platform instead of finding some random indie blog on your own. If they’re so good at connecting us together, how come the internet so divisive today? Do you really even gaf about all the people you follow on social media? We’ve lost the core principle of the internet as a place for community and true connection.

(Watch Hackers and SLC Punk)

Parimal Satyal - Against a User Hostile Web

I recently turned off ad settings in my google account and I saw the demographic profile they made for me. I think I input some of that information myself when I first made my account years ago, but some information was generated by investigating my activity. I know that most websites are collecting our data and storing cookies, but I didn’t know that the number would actually be so high. It makes a lot of sense why advertisers are able to get such a good profile of our demographic and intimate details of our identities. I feel like companies collecting our information see it like collecting drops of our sweat–it’s something that we naturally excrete and we just let it drip away or dry off. If we aren’t using it, why can’t these companies collect them? Like our sweat, it’s an incredible violation of privacy because they’re pretty much collecting a piece of our bodies, our digital dna. This information can be so easily used against us and now we are vulnerable against our will to things like identity theft, data leaks, and targeted advertising. It’s another form of selling our bodies under capitalism, except our data isn’t even seen as our property to give away to begin with. And it’s harder to understand and regulate because it’s such a new technological problem and governments benefit from this kind of mass surveillance and it’s difficult for the average user to conceptualize the internet as something real and increasingly dangerous rather than a bunch of beep boops in the cloud.

8/27

JR Carpenter - Handmade Web

There’s an ongoing movement of people who have taken a step back from digital technologies or using older forms of these technologies because they’re overwhelmed or disinterested in how the web has become kinda mass-manufactured and “sloppified” (especially after AI). Instead of completely erasing your digital footprint, the ideology behind handmade web would be a great middle ground. At the core of the movement, people are nostalgic for a simpler time and hope to generate a more intentional relationship with our digital technologies. I think it’s interesting how Carpenter points out that iPads only work because we don’t know how they work. The knowledge of how the web/computers work has become more guarded and unapproachable. Because we don’t know how things work, big corporations can in turn take advantage of our ignorance and make money off of us as we become more helpless. Perhaps, in actively partaking in creation on the web, we can reclaim some of that autonomy and empower ourselves to exist on the web.

Laurel Schwulst - My Website is a Shifting House Next to a River of Knowledge. What Could Yours be?
Ursula K Le Guin - A Rant on Technology

The web is organic and ephemeral. I love the metaphor of website as puddle because I love the space that it gives for the web to also decay and disappear. The web is such a powerful technology that has changed our lives, and as humans we sometimes think that technology will help us create immortality–like storing our consciousness in the cloud after we die–but like our flesh bodies, the web isn’t forever. From a reading in my creative coding class, they describe the distinction of mechanical and organic–mechanical as being constructed by separate parts and organic as something that builds outward from within–then describing generative art as the crossroads between the two. I think these handmade websites also exist on this boundary.
